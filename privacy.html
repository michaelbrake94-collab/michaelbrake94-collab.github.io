Hobbs privacy policy

Hobbs is a private assistant with one user, its owner. It is not offered to anyone else, so the only Google account it handles is the owner's own.

What Hobbs accesses

When the owner asks, Hobbs reads and searches email in the owner's Gmail account, saves drafts adds or removes the owner's labels, moves labelled email out of the inbox, and moves email the owner chooses to Trash. It does not send email or delete it permanently. Before drafting to someone, it reads the owner's own recent emails to that person, so the draft matches how the owner writes. If the owner turns it on, Hobbs also does this each morning by itself: it reads the last day's email and saves draft replies for the owner to review, and it still never sends them. If the owner turns it on, Hobbs also looks at their calendar, their list of commitments and their recent email by itself a few times a day, and sends the owner a short note when something needs them in the next few hours.

When the owner asks, Hobbs also reads events in the owner's main Google Calendar, including the names, email addresses and replies of the people on them, books events, changes or deletes events it made, and changes the colour of events. After the owner approves each one, it sends invites, changes and cancellations to the guests the owner chooses, and Google emails those guests the event's details.

When the owner asks, Hobbs also searches and reads documents in the owner's Google Drive, including files shared with them. It cannot change, share or delete what is already there. Once the owner approves each batch, Hobbs saves attachments from their email into a folder of its own in their Drive. If the owner turns it on, Hobbs also sorts their inbox by itself each hour: it reads email and its attachments, labels email and moves finished email out of the inbox, and saves documents such as property statements and invoices into the owner's own Drive folders. It only adds files to their Drive; it never changes, moves, shares or deletes anything already there.

Where the data goes

The Google sign-in tokens, Hobbs's conversation log, its lists of the owner's open decisions and commitments, and its notes on the people, places and projects in the owner's life (such as a contact's name, email address and role), which can include details from email, calendar or documents, are stored on the owner's home computer. So is a copy of the owner's own words from their sent email, used to write a guide to their writing style. The guide is a document in the owner's Google Drive, which the owner edits. To answer a request, the email, calendar or document content involved is sent to Anthropic's Claude service under the owner's own Claude account. If the owner turns on notifications, Hobbs's short notes reach the owner's own phone through the phone maker's push service (Apple's, for an iPhone), encrypted so that service can't read them. To give weather and travel times, the location written on an upcoming calendar event (only that, never its title, guests or notes) is sent to OpenStreetMap's place search to find where it is, map coordinates are sent to the Open-Meteo weather service, and, if the owner turns on travel times, the start and end places are sent to Google Maps. If the owner sets it up, their phone tells Hobbs when they arrive at or leave home or work and where they are; this is kept on the home computer for 30 days, and their current position is sent to Open-Meteo or Google Maps only to give weather or travel time from where they are. This content is not sold, used for advertising or shared with anyone else.

Google user data

Hobbs's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Removing access

The owner can end Hobbs's access at any time from their Google account permissions, and can delete the stored tokens and conversation log from their computer.
